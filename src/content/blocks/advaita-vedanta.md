For Advaita, the self (ātman) and ultimate reality (brahman) are one:cite[wiki].:note[“Self” here means not the personality but the awareness that witnesses it; Advaita holds that this awareness is mistaken for the body and mind it observes:cite[wiki].] Sangeetha Menon, in the IEP, takes the word's literal sense to be “non-secondness” rather than the usual “non-dualism”:cite[iep]. It is one school of [Vedānta](/vedanta) among several, not the outlook of Hinduism as a whole; most Hindus do not follow it:cite[wiki]. Its standing as the model of Hindu thought owes much to the 14th-century Advaitin Vidyāraṇya, who ranked it above every other system, and to the nineteenth-century Western scholars who followed his lead:cite[wiki].

Why, then, does each of us seem a separate, limited person? Śaṅkara's answer is ignorance (avidyā), which shows itself as a mutual “superimposition” (adhyāsa): we credit the self with the features of body and mind, and body and mind with the self's:cite[sep]. Ignorance has no beginning, and he thought it fruitless to ask where it came from:cite[iep]. In classical Advaita its cosmic counterpart is [māyā](/maya), the power by which [brahman](/brahman) appears as a world of many things:cite[iep]. Advaita therefore speaks from two standpoints: the conventional (vyāvahārika), where the shared world of persons and things holds good, and the ultimate (pāramārthika), where there is only brahman:cite[sep].

Liberation (mokṣa) is then not something to be produced: the self already is brahman, and what frees it is knowledge (jñāna) that removes the mistake:cite[sep]. Against Maṇḍana Miśra and others who joined knowledge with ritual works, Śaṅkara held that action can only prepare the mind:cite[iep]. The knowledge is sparked by the Upaniṣads' statements of identity, such as “you are that”, and whoever gains it is free while still alive (jīvanmukti, “liberation while living”):cite[sep].:note[Śaṅkara illustrates with the tenth man. Ten children cross a river; each counts the group, leaves himself out, finds nine and mourns the one presumed drowned. Told “you are the tenth”, nobody gains anything new; an error simply falls away:cite[sep].]

The school is older than Śaṅkara, who refers to a tradition before him:cite[iep]. Its oldest complete surviving text is Gauḍapāda's Māṇḍūkya-kārikā:cite[wiki], dated anywhere from the early 6th to the 7th century:cite[wiki-gaudapada]; tradition makes Gauḍapāda the teacher of Śaṅkara's teacher:cite[sep]. [Śaṅkara](/sankara) systematised Advaita in the 8th century rather than founding it:cite[sep], though Richard King finds his works overshadowed until about the 10th by those of his older contemporary Maṇḍana Miśra:cite[wiki-sankara]. His successors split over where ignorance resides: in the individual soul (jīva), for the Bhāmatī school of Vācaspati Miśra, or in brahman, for the Vivaraṇa school of Prakāśātman, each school taking its name from the commentary at its root:cite[iep].

Other Vedāntins attacked Advaita for centuries, from Bhāskara, in whom Andrew Nicholson finds many stock objections first stated, to Rāmānuja and Madhva:cite[iep-bhedabheda]. Its longest quarrel was with Dvaita. Vyāsatīrtha, whose arguments drew on [Navya-Nyāya](/navya-nyaya) among other schools, attacked it in the Nyāyāmṛta; Madhusūdana Sarasvatī answered line by line in the Advaitasiddhi, and further replies and rejoinders followed before the dispute died down:cite[wiki-vyasatirtha].
